In their words

Ownership, board and staff

Fact-checking, corrections and code of ethics

As a family-owned newspaper with roots dating back to 1789, The Berkshire Eagle is long considered to be one of the best small-market newspapers in the United States, reporting on the Berkshire Hills of western Massachusetts with depth, clarity and fairness. The Eagle's reporting has helped to establish the region as a prime cultural and recreational destination. Our commitment to excellence has attracted many promising, young journalists, many of whom have moved on to the national stage.

Now owned by a group of civic-minded owners with local roots, The Eagle is fully re-committed to the goal of journalistic excellence, with the vision of being the best community news organization in America. We are proud of our storied and ongoing heritage as a printed newspaper, and we have built that pride into a strong and durable presence online too.

Both in print and online, we surprise, delight and inform our readers for whom The Eagle is an essential part of their day. We publish compelling, comprehensive and trustworthy Berkshire-focused news and information. We believe that democracy requires a town square, and that The Eagle is the town square for the Berkshires.

In addition to a five-day printed newspaper, The Berkshire Eagle is published online via a website and an app, and it distributes a suite of daily and weekly newsletters. Also affiliated with The Eagle are:

The B, a quarterly magazine covering Berkshire culture, recreation and lifestyles;

BerkshiresWeek.com, a go-to digital resource for tourists and residents alike;

Berkshire Business Journal, a monthly print publication covering the commercial sector in the Berkshires;

The Southern Berkshire Shoppers' Guide, a weekly mailed to every household in the greater southern Berkshire market; and

Eagle Printing, Binding & Mailing, a commercial printing division that prints and distributes newsprint products throughout New England and upstate New York.

Our organization is supported not only by subscriptions, advertising and commercial printing, but also by philanthropic contributions, large and small, from readers and institutions who value our efforts. The Berkshire Eagle Local Journalism Fund, managed by the Berkshire-Taconic Community Foundation, supports our reporting of local matters that relate to health, education, economic development, and arts and culture. Your donation will help ensure sustainability of quality, independent journalism in the Berkshires.

Their commitment, innovative spirit and skills have made The Eagle an industry-leading news organization recognized both in the Berkshires and far beyond. We're always on the lookout for talent.

The Berkshire Eagle traces its origins to The Western Star, a weekly newspaper founded in 1789 in Stockbridge, Mass. Although that paper and its successors changed ownership, name and place of publication often, it was a continuously operated newspaper enterprise, which eventually became the weekly Berkshire County Eagle in 1852.

Kelton B. Miller, along with several partners, purchased The Berkshire County Eagle in 1891. The following year, on May 9, 1892, it began daily publication as The Berkshire Evening Eagle, and Miller soon bought out his partners. (The Berkshire County Eagle, however, remained a part of the paper, as a section within the Wednesday edition of the daily, until 1953.)

The Miller family retained ownership until 1995. After Kelton Bedell Miller died in 1941, ownership passed to his sons, Lawrence K. "Pete" Miller and Donald B. Miller, as editor and publisher, respectively.

The Miller brothers focused on hiring talent and building the quality of The Eagle's newsroom. The newspaper became known as a great place for graduates of journalism schools to begin their careers, and many of those reporters went on to renowned careers throughout the journalistic world at publications such as The New York Times, The Washington Post, The Wall Street Journal and Time magazine.

Press critic Ben Bagdikian stated in 1972 that there were only three great newspapers in the world, each great in its own way: The New York Times, Le Monde, and The Berkshire Eagle. The Washington Post, where he had served as editor and ombudsman, he said at the time, was “not yet a great paper.”

In a 1973 Time magazine article about The Eagle, then Boston Globe editor Thomas Winship is quoted as calling The Eagle the best newspaper “of its size in the country.” At the time, the paper had nearly 20 local columnists, 23 stringers and a news staff of 35, considered large for a paper its size. (Circulation was 32,000 at the time.)

In 1973, The Eagle's editorial page editor, Roger B. Linscott, won a Pulitzer Prize for editorials on local, state and national topics written during 1972.

Over the years, the Millers acquired daily and weekly newspapers in nearby markets in Vermont and Connecticut. The Eagle launched a Sunday edition in 1987.

In 1989, the Millers chose to renovate, as a new headquarters and printing plant for their company, a factory building complex in Pittsfield, originally the Eaton, Crane & Pike Company factory. As a result of a recession, the company was unable to service the debt it had assumed to finance this project. Consequently, in 1995 the Millers sold their holdings to MediaNews Group, a company founded by William Dean Singleton of Denver, Colorado.

The following year, MediaNews added the North Adams Transcript to its western New England holdings. In January 2014, the Transcript ceased operations and was merged into The Eagle.

Under MediaNews Group's management, as newspapers in general faced increasing financial challenges there were multiple rounds of staff reductions as various functions were consolidated into centralized locations on a regional or national basis. Especially after the 2008 financial crisis, advertising and readership fell year after year. Following a bankruptcy proceeding, MediaNews Group was acquired by Alden Global Capital, a hedge fund that focused on distressed assets; Alden operated MediaNews Group together with the former Journal-Register Company as Digital First Media.

Return to local ownership

In April 2016, a team of local investors bought The Eagle, along with its Vermont sister newspapers the Bennington Banner, Brattleboro Reformer and Manchester Journal, from Digital First Media . The investor team consisted of former Visa Inc. President John C. "Hans" Morris, local retired judge Fredric D. Rutberg, M&T Bank Chairman Robert G. Wilmers and Stanford Lipsey, former publisher of The Buffalo News and former owner of the Omaha Sun newspaper group of Nebraska. Lipsey died November 1, 2016. Wilmers died in December, 2017.

In introducing the new ownership and its goals to The Eagle's readership, Rutberg wrote: “The goal is to make The Eagle a part of the finest community newspaper group in America,” Rutberg wrote. “Our business plan is simple. By improving the quality and quantity of the content in our publications, we expect to increase our readership which will, in turn, increase our revenues, and ensure the future of these publications.”

Under the new owners, The Eagle has been able to hire additional newsroom staff, expanded its investigative team, and has launched new content. The Eagle's group of products now includes The B, a quarterly glossy magazine, Berkshire Business Journal, a monthly tabloid business newspaper, and the Southern Berkshire Shopper's Guide, a weekly paper based in Great Barrington, Mass.

Recently, The Eagle invested in a new printing press, an office renovation, and new computer systems for all content and production functions. The paper has a community advisory board that includes representatives of many local nonprofits and businesses, as well as nationally recognized journalists, authors and artists with Berkshire area connections.

Since 2016, the combined readership of The Eagle's print and digital editions has stabilized and begun to grow, and the paper has received a variety of awards and honors for the quality of its content.

In October 2020, in response to economic challenges associated with the COVID-19 pandemic (but also as a step toward the possibility of digital-only publication in the future), The Eagle reduced its print frequency to five days per week, Tuesday through Saturday, with the traditional Sunday package of supplements and inserts moving into the Saturday slot. On Mondays, while there is no printed paper, an electronic facsimile of a printed newspaper is available, and the paper's website is updated seven days a week.

In May 2021, The Eagle company sold its Vermont newspapers to Vermont News and Media LLC, and is now fully focused on producing and distributing content focused on the Berkshires.

The Berkshire Eagle editorial board consists of its president/publisher, executive editor and editorial page editor, and meets regularly. The editorial board meets with key officeholders, political candidates, civic leaders, policy experts and others invited to present their views during on-the-record meetings.

The editorial pages are managed separately but in parallel with the news-gathering staff, and have the mission of encouraging the people of Berkshire County to take steps to improve the quality of life and economic conditions for all, to vigorously exercise and protect the First Amendment, and to be the voice of the Berkshires without fear or favor. Alongside our own views expressed in editorials, we strive to present a wide variety of opinions and viewpoints in letters to the editor and op-ed columns.

The editorial board formulates the guiding editorial positions and ensures that these positions are stated clearly. The Eagle's editorials reflect the Berkshires' history, heritage, culture and entrepreneurial spirit.

The Eagle's editorials catalyze change, spur civil discourse, reward greatness and inspire action among the people of Berkshire County. The Eagle's opinions, while stated boldly, are consistent with its public service mission and strive to be the voice of reason. The Eagle's editorials:

Are regarded as well-researched with sourcing from our news stories, news services and our own independent sources;

Are an independent voice on issues and never do not the entire platform of political or partisan group;

Add unique, persuasive points to current discourse on topics;

Lead by taking positions urgently and seizing on opportunities in the local news to repeat and refine positions on our agenda;

Are refined through collegial editorial board review prior to publication and seek excellence in every presentation; and,

Use constructive points to encourage local, state, regional and national leaders.

As such, The Eagle's editorial voice and conscience is seen as the main convener and connector on issues of vital local, regional and statewide importance, including:

Civility in life and discourse;

Education, local and national;

Local economic development issues;

Environmental issues; and,

Questions before local, state and national leaders and legislative bodies.

The Eagle's editorial values include intellectual honesty, integrity and responsibility. Recognizing the impact The Eagle's editorial positions have in the community, the board gathers accurate information and examines in depth all sides of the issues on which we editorialize. The editorial page and the editorial board function independently from the reporting functions of the newsroom.

The Eagle has taken certain key editorial positions consistently, and the editorial board will endorse changes to those positions only after deep discussion and research leading to consensus. These positions include a general predisposition toward free expression, as well as leaning toward progressive ideas, environmental conservation, the encouragement of innovation and entrepreneurship, and the promotion of tourism and cultural entities.
