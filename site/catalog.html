Tampa Bay Times

St. Petersburg, FL · covers Tampa Bay · metro daily owned by a nonprofit (the Poynter Institute) · est. 1884 · details · RSS

The Times established a newsroom AI Committee to provide guidance on the responsible use of artificial intelligence while remaining firm in our values of accuracy and accountability. Reporters have long used AI-powered tools for workflow tasks, like transcribing interview audio. As AI capabilities advance, we will explore ways it can empower our work. Examples might include using AI to parse large datasets or aid in translation. AI use requires a critical eye and oversight. As we would with any source, we treat its insights as unverified until proven, and reporters may not use AI to directly craft content published in the Times. When AI-powered reporting or analysis is key to a story, we commit to offering transparency to readers about why and how such tools were used. There’s a fine balance — we must interrogate and explore this powerful technology, but we also must keep in mind its biases, privacy concerns and potential for errors. AI tools will not interfere with our integrity as independent writers and thinkers.

Invisible Institute

Chicago, IL · covers Chicago — police accountability and civic power · nonprofit investigative newsroom · details

The Invisible Institute arose out of the work of executive director Jamie Kalven during the final chapter of high-rise public housing in Chicago. From 1994 until the final demolition in 2007, Kalven’s base of operations was the Stateway Gardens public housing development, said on the basis of the 1990 census to be the single poorest community in the nation. Kalven had several roles there. He established a program of "grassroots public works" aimed at creating alternatives for ex-offenders and members of street gangs. He served as advisor to the resident council, participating in negotiations with the Chicago Housing Authority, Department of Housing and Urban Development, and private developers. And he practiced a form of human rights documentation that Studs Terkel once characterized as "guerrilla journalism."
